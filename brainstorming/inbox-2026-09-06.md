# Inbox — Drew's notes, 2026-09-06

Raw notes from a play session, verbatim, with my read on each. Nothing here is a decision. Items move
to `docs/` (or a milestone) when they are; the rest stay until pruned.

## Presentation and feel

**Show a unique effect for deck Marks when they fire.** Today a Mark firing emits `mark-fired` and the
card gets a glyph, but every Mark looks the same when it goes off. Each of the fourteen wants its own
short signature — Lantern a warm bloom, Tithe a coin flick, Echo a second ghost card, Twin a wire drawn
between the pair. The bus already carries `mark` and `depth`; the presenter has everything it needs, so
this is `table/` work with no rules change. Depth could ride the intensity: a chain three deep should
look like a chain.

**Auto-new-hand after a timer when the Dealer wins.** The Dealer already deals a fresh hand, but only
when asked. A short, visible countdown after a win — cancellable by touching the felt — is the idle
reading of "the table resets itself while you watch". Careful: the win cascade wants its beat first,
so the timer starts when the celebration ends, not when `hand-won` fires.

**'Deck' deserves a real design.** Agreed, and it is the most-visited panel: it is where the 52
generators live, where Marks are placed, and where the whole economy is legible at a glance. Ideas: the
grid as an actual spread deck rather than a table; charge shown as ink density rather than a number;
suits as columns with their own weight; the Marks tray as a drawer beneath. This is the panel that
should make someone say "oh, it's a deck".

## Progression and upgrades

**More interesting layout and organisation of upgrades.** Nineteen in two tiers is already a wall of
buttons. Grouping by what they *do* (what wakes cards, what charges them, what pays out, what bends
rules) beats grouping by tier.

**Maxed upgrades move to the bottom of the list.** Cheap, obviously right, and it keeps the live
decisions in reach. Do this one soon — it is a half-hour of work that improves every session after it.

**Upgrade trees — "I don't love this direction."** Noted, and I agree: a tree competes with the
Constellation, which is already the tree-shaped layer. But the *grouping* underneath the idea is worth
keeping: suit-focused, number-focused, face-card-focused families of upgrades, with challenges attached.
Families without a tree: the grouping gives identity, the Constellation keeps the branching.

**Recipes.** Offer a casual player a named bundle on a win — "Deal the Suits", "Bank the Aces" — that
buys or maxes a set of upgrades in one press, so they never have to grind the buttons. Guardrails: only
in manual modes, and limited (a few per run, or one per win) so an aficionado who *likes* the buttons is
not taxed for it. This is the single best "few relaxing hands a day" affordance on the list.

## Documentation

**ELI5 rulebook.** A plain-language explanation of what the game is and how to play it, for someone who
has never seen an incremental. In-game, not a doc: the Keeper explaining the table.

**Master game handbook.** The complete reference — every upgrade, Mark, node, numbering system, game, and
what each actually does. This is `docs/`, and it should be generated from `content/` where possible so it
cannot drift.

**Gameplay flow diagrams.** Hand -> home -> charge -> spark -> shuffle -> Cut -> Reshuffle -> Ascend, as
one picture. Mermaid in `docs/`, so it lives beside the design it describes.

**Storyline diagram.** The Keeper's notes, the milestones, and the constellation lore laid out in the
order a player meets them. Reveals what is thin: the middle of the journey has fewer moments than the
start or the end.
